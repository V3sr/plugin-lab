# Organizer interview guide

Goal: establish a recurring task and willingness to change a workflow. This is a guide, not evidence that any interviews have happened.

## Recruiting criteria

Recruit people who organized an event, coordinated a team or handed off responsibilities in the past month. Include at least one team outside the founder's existing network. Ask whether they can authorize a bot, who else must approve, and whether members would accept the data flow.

Keep interview notes in private storage with consent. This public repository should contain only aggregate findings or participant-approved anonymized excerpts. Do not record member messages or names merely to make a demo realistic.

## Twenty-minute conversation

1. Tell me about the last event or project you coordinated through chat.
2. Which decision was hardest to find afterward? Walk me through how you found it.
3. What was the consequence of missing or misunderstanding it?
4. How often does this happen? What happened in the previous event?
5. What tool or person solves it now? What does that cost in attention or effort?
6. When someone changes a plan, how do you know the previous plan is outdated?
7. How do outgoing organizers hand work to incoming people?
8. Which channels must never be included? Who should be allowed to see a brief?
9. Could your team authorize an installation? What security or institutional review is needed?
10. Where would you use this: ChatGPT, Discord, or a web page? Why?

Only after those questions, show the synthetic brief. Ask the organizer to identify an error, verify a cited claim and find an unresolved task. Then ask what action they would take from the result.

## Concrete pilot request

Ask whether they will try it around a named upcoming event, with a bounded set of approved channels and transparent data notice. Request a second session after the next event. An enthusiastic compliment is weaker evidence than a scheduled trial and voluntary return.

## Record privately

Recent problem; frequency; workaround; consequences; exact task; installation authority; data concerns; preferred interface; explicit pilot commitment; next contact permission. Do not count messages sent as interviews completed.

## Compare alternative concepts fairly

Show three small, equally concrete examples: an event decision brief, a learning-page QA report and a creator experiment review. Ask participants only about workflows they perform. Do not choose a concept because friends politely prefer the founder's first idea.

## Synthesis template for a public update

“We interviewed [N] organizers across [N] independent teams. [N] described [specific recent recurring problem]. [N] committed to a bounded pilot. The strongest concern was [aggregate concern]. We changed [design decision]. These numbers describe this sample and do not estimate the whole market.”
